QAT vs normal fine-tuning for FP8 deployment

Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct fine-tuned on GSM8K (grade-school maths)

1. What are we solving?

Large language models are often converted to 8-bit floating point (FP8) to make them smaller for deployment, but the conversion rounds every weight and usually costs some accuracy.

Quantization-aware training (QAT) applies the same FP8 rounding while the model is being fine-tuned, so the model can adapt to it. We test whether a model fine-tuned with QAT is more accurate after FP8 conversion than a model fine-tuned normally in BF16.

2. Architecture: how we solve it

Two models are fine-tuned independently from the same starting model on the same data. The only difference is the training step: the QAT model also sees FP8 fake quantization in its forward pass, while its weights stay in BF16. Both are then converted to FP8 with the same tool and evaluated on the same test questions.

The training step is the only difference between the two pipelines. Every other step is identical.
